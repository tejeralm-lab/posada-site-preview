What We Did

Three problems, solved.

The homeowners had two vanities, a bathtub they had no use for and nowhere to set things down in the morning. Mike took the tub out and ran one long vanity along that wall, with drawer bases and cabinet bases below and a much longer countertop above, and left the end of the run open so natural light reaches the room.

The toilet room was 30 inches wide. Sitting down, the door hit your knee, so it had to stay open. The wall moved out to 48 inches, and the door closes now.

  • Bathtub removed; one long vanity with drawer and cabinet bases in its place
  • More countertop for morning prep, with the end of the run left open for natural light
  • Toilet room widened from 30 inches to 48 so the door closes
  • Curbless walk-in shower, pitched so it drains properly
  • 2 by 4 foot matte tile on the floor, 2 by 4 foot gloss tile on the walls
  • Infinity niche with mitered, epoxied edges and no metal trim
  • Half wall capped in quartzite with a waterfall edge
  • Towel warmer beside the shower door

Details That Matter

What Cameron points out inside the shower.

The parts of a shower you only notice when they are done wrong, explained by the tile installer himself.

01

Curbless, pitched to drain

The curb is gone, so the floor tile runs straight into the shower with nothing to step over. The shower floor is pitched at the proper angle so water goes where it should, and the hex mosaic carries a square tile-in drain that lifts out and sits right back in place.

02

An infinity niche, no metal

The niche in the back wall is built with mitered edges and finished with epoxy, so there is no metal trim breaking up the tile. The result reads more modern, and nothing interrupts the line of the wall.

03

Leveling clips, no lippage

Large-format tile is very hard to set flat without them. Clips every six to ten inches, depending on the material, keep every edge flush so there is no lippage, and the inside corners are finished in silicone.

04

Quartzite waterfall

The half wall between the shower and the room is capped in quartzite that continues down the end as a waterfall, with epoxied edges in place of visible seams. Custom work, and the miters came out clean.
