Do You Need a Permit to Remodel a Kitchen or Bathroom in Florida?

By Mike Posada·July 13, 2026·Updated July 27, 2026·5 min read

Do you need a permit to remodel a bathroom or kitchen in Central Florida? For most everyday remodeling, the answer is no. Updating cabinets, countertops, tile, fixtures, and finishes generally does not require a permit. Permits mainly come into play when a project changes the structure of your home or its exterior. This plain-English guide covers when a permit is (and isn’t) needed across Orange, Seminole, and the surrounding counties, and how Posada handles those projects.

When a remodeling permit is required in Florida

The good news for most homeowners: standard kitchen and bathroom remodeling updates what is already there and does not require a permit. Permits come into play mainly when a project changes the structure of the home or its exterior.

Work that usually does not need a permit (the bulk of remodeling):

  • New cabinets, countertops, and hardware
  • Replacing a vanity, sink, faucet, or fixtures in the same spot
  • New tile, flooring, or a backsplash
  • Fresh paint and finishes

Work that does require a permit:

  • Removing or altering a load-bearing wall or beam
  • Exterior changes — adding a new window or door, or making an existing one larger
  • Other structural changes to the home

What a permit typically costs (when one is needed)

When a project does require a permit, the fee is usually a small share of the overall budget and is based on the value of the work. The ranges below are typical estimates for the structural and exterior work that triggers a permit — your local building department sets the exact fee.

Fees vary by jurisdiction and project value, so treat these as planning numbers. If your project needs a permit, we make sure the costs are clear up front.

Permit rules by county and city

Each jurisdiction runs its own building department with its own fees, forms, and timelines. A project in Orange County may follow a slightly different process than the same job in Seminole County or a city with its own department.

  • Orange County — covers Orlando’s unincorporated areas, Windermere-area homes, and more
  • Seminole County — covers Lake Mary, Longwood, and surrounding areas
  • City departments — Winter Park and several other cities issue their own permits

Because requirements and review times differ, it pays to plan your remodel with professionals who know how each local department works.

Why permits protect you

A permit is not just paperwork. It means an inspector verifies the work meets the Florida Building Code. That protects your safety, your home’s value, and your insurance coverage.

Skipping permits can create real problems down the road:

  • Trouble selling — buyers and appraisers flag unpermitted work
  • Denied insurance claims if unpermitted work is involved
  • Fines or orders to remove and redo the work
  • Safety risks from plumbing or wiring that was never inspected

How Posada handles permits

Posada Remodeling & Design specializes in kitchen and bathroom remodeling — cabinets, countertops, tile, fixtures, and finishes. The large majority of this work does not require a permit, so most projects move straight into design and installation.

When a project does require a permit — such as removing a load-bearing wall or beam, or an exterior change like adding or enlarging a window or door — we bring in our licensed general contractor partner. That partner pulls the permit and manages and oversees that portion of the work, so it stays with the right professional and is completed to code.

Either way, we tell you up front whether your project will need a permit — so there are no surprises once the work starts.

How long does the permit process take?

Timelines depend on the jurisdiction and how busy the building department is. Simple trade permits can be issued quickly, while remodels that need plan review take longer. As a rough guide for Central Florida:

  • Over-the-counter permits — often same day to a few days for straightforward plumbing or electrical work
  • Permits needing plan review — commonly one to three weeks
  • Inspections — scheduled at key stages (rough-in and final) as the work progresses

Building permitting into your schedule early keeps the project moving and avoids last-minute delays.

Steps to a smooth remodel

A little planning up front makes the permit process painless. Here is the path most Central Florida remodels follow:

  1. Define the scope — decide what is changing and whether any structural or exterior work is involved
  2. Confirm whether the project needs a permit at all (most remodeling does not)
  3. If it does, bring in our licensed general contractor partner to prepare plans and applications
  4. That partner pulls the required permit and oversees that portion of the work
  5. Complete the work and pass any required inspections
  6. Enjoy a remodel that is done right — and documented and inspected where needed

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a permit to remodel a bathroom in Florida?

Usually not. Most bathroom remodeling — new tile, a vanity, fixtures, and finishes — does not require a permit. A permit is needed mainly for structural changes or exterior work, such as adding or enlarging a window.

Can I remodel a kitchen without a permit?

Yes — most kitchen remodeling (new cabinets, countertops, tile, and fixtures) does not require a permit. Permits apply mainly to structural changes like removing a load-bearing wall, or exterior changes like a new or larger window or door.

Does Posada pull the permits?

Posada is a design-build remodeling studio, so we do not pull permits for the everyday remodeling work that does not require one. When a project does need a permit — structural or exterior work — we coordinate it through our licensed general contractor partner, who pulls the permit and oversees that part of the job.
