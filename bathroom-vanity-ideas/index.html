Bathrooms

Bathroom vanity ideas for every style and budget.

By Mike Posada · September 21, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · 7 min read

In Short

Choose a vanity by answering three questions first: who uses the room, how much space you really have and what needs to live in it. Floating vanities open up the floor, furniture-style vanities add warmth, and double vanities suit shared primary bathrooms. In Central Florida humidity, plywood boxes, sealed cutouts and a vented exhaust fan keep a vanity looking new.

Key facts

  • Keeping the drain and supply lines in place keeps a vanity swap simple; moving them adds plumbing, tile and drywall work.
  • Plywood boxes hold up better than particleboard, which can swell when it gets wet.
  • A vanity is usually one line item within a larger bathroom remodel, itemized in your proposal.

The vanity sets the tone for the whole bathroom. It is the first thing you see when you walk in, the place you start and end every day, and usually the hardest-working piece of storage in the room.

Here are the bathroom vanity ideas our designers recommend most for Central Florida homes, from floating modern looks to warm furniture-style classics, plus the sizing, counter and hardware choices that make each one work.

How to choose a bathroom vanity before you pick a style

Style is the fun part, but three practical questions narrow the field fast. Answer them first and the right vanity ideas usually become obvious.

  1. Who uses the room? A primary bath shared by two people every morning has very different needs from a powder room guests use twice a week.
  2. How much space do you really have? Measure wall to wall, and leave room for the door swing, the toilet and a comfortable path in front of the vanity.
  3. What needs to live in it? Hair tools, towels and backup supplies need drawers and outlets. A powder room needs almost nothing.
  4. Will the plumbing stay put? Keeping the drain and supply lines where they are keeps a vanity swap simple. Moving them opens up more options but adds work.

Floating vanities for a modern, open feel

A floating vanity mounts to the wall and leaves the floor open underneath. That strip of visible tile makes a bathroom feel larger than it is, which is why it is one of our most requested ideas for compact baths.

Floating vanities also make cleaning easier, since there is no base to mop around. Add a strip of LED lighting under the cabinet and you get a soft night light with a high-end hotel feel.

The catch is structure. The wall needs solid blocking behind the drywall, and the plumbing should be planned so the drain sits inside the cabinet footprint. For more space-saving ideas, see our guide to small bathroom remodel ideas.

Furniture-style vanities with warmth and character

A furniture-style vanity looks like a chest or dresser that happens to hold a sink. Turned legs, inset doors and a rich stain or painted finish give traditional and transitional bathrooms real character.

These work especially well in older Winter Park and College Park homes, where a sleek slab cabinet can feel out of place. Pair one with a framed mirror and classic sconces and the room feels collected rather than builder-grade.

Double vanity ideas for shared primary bathrooms

Double vanities remain the top request for primary bathrooms, and for good reason. Two sinks end the morning traffic jam, and each person gets a drawer stack of their own.

You do not always need two full sinks to get the benefit. On shorter walls, a single wide sink with generous counter space on both sides, or one sink plus a seated makeup area, can serve two people better than two cramped basins.

  • Separate towers: a tall linen cabinet between two sinks adds storage and gives each person privacy.
  • Split vanities: two separate vanities on facing or adjacent walls work well in larger primary suites.
  • Makeup station: a lowered section of counter with a stool turns part of the vanity into a seated grooming area.

Vanity colors, counters and hardware

Color is where a vanity starts to feel personal. Navy, deep green and warm wood tones are the most popular choices we see right now, especially against white walls and brass or aged-gold hardware.

White and light oak remain timeless if you plan to sell within a few years. A painted vanity in a bold color is an easy way to add personality without committing to colored tile.

Choosing a vanity countertop

Quartz tops offer the best mix of durability and low maintenance for most bathrooms. They resist stains from makeup and toiletries and never need sealing. Granite and marble bring natural character but need more care.

If you are weighing stone options, our quartz vs. granite countertops guide covers the trade-offs, and our guide to countertop finishes explains polished, honed and leathered surfaces.

Sinks and faucets

Undermount sinks give the cleanest look and the easiest counter to wipe down. Vessel sinks make a statement in powder rooms but need a shorter vanity or a taller faucet to keep the height comfortable.

Wall-mounted faucets free up counter space and pair beautifully with floating vanities, but they have to be planned before the wall is closed up. A floating vanity is also a cornerstone of a calm, open room; see our spa bathroom ideas for the rest of that look.

Bathroom vanity ideas that hold up in Florida humidity

Central Florida bathrooms are hard on cabinetry. Steam from daily showers, humid summers and the occasional splash all work on the vanity box, especially along the bottom edge and around the sink cutout.

  • Choose moisture-resistant construction. Plywood boxes and quality finishes hold up far better than particleboard, which can swell when it gets wet.
  • Seal every cutout. Sink and plumbing openings should be sealed so water cannot wick into raw edges.
  • Plan the ventilation. A properly sized exhaust fan protects the vanity, the paint and the grout.
  • Leave a gap from the tub or shower. A little distance keeps the vanity out of the direct splash zone.

Storage ideas that make a vanity work harder

The best vanity ideas are often inside the cabinet. Deep drawers beat doors for most people, because you can see everything at once instead of digging into a dark cabinet.

  • U-shaped drawers that wrap around the plumbing so no space is wasted
  • In-drawer outlets so hair tools stay plugged in and out of sight
  • Pull-out organizers for cleaning supplies under the sink
  • Shallow top drawers with dividers for makeup and daily essentials
  • A tall side cabinet or open shelf for towels near the vanity

If someone in the home has limited mobility, a vanity with an open knee space and lever handles can make the bathroom far easier to use. Our aging-in-place bathroom remodel guide covers this in detail.

What a new vanity means for your remodel budget

A vanity swap that keeps the plumbing in place is one of the simplest bathroom upgrades. Moving the sink, adding a second one or switching to a wall-mounted faucet adds plumbing work and usually new tile and drywall repair.

Bathroom vanity ideas FAQs

What is the most popular bathroom vanity style right now?

Floating vanities and furniture-style vanities are the two most requested styles in our Central Florida projects. Floating suits modern and small bathrooms, while furniture-style suits traditional and transitional homes.

How wide should a double vanity be?

Plan on about 60 inches as a practical minimum for two sinks, and 72 inches or more if you want comfortable counter space for each person.

Is a floating vanity a good idea for a small bathroom?

Yes. Seeing the floor underneath makes the room feel bigger, and it is easier to clean. Just plan the wall blocking and plumbing before installation.

What is the best countertop for a bathroom vanity?

Quartz is the best all-round choice for most homeowners because it is durable, stain resistant and never needs sealing.

Can I replace a vanity without redoing the whole bathroom?

Often, yes, if the new vanity fits the same plumbing locations and covers the old footprint on the floor and wall.
