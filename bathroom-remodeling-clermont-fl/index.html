Good Questions

Clermont bathroom questions.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

Industry guides put most bathroom remodels at roughly one to four weeks of construction, with primary suites that move plumbing or walls at the longer end. Ordering tile, vanities and glass before demolition is what keeps the schedule.

What types of bathrooms do you remodel?

Primary bathrooms, guest bathrooms, powder rooms, walk-in showers and full custom bathroom spaces: from a tub-to-shower conversion with frameless glass to a spa-style primary suite.

Should I choose a walk-in shower or a tub for my bathroom remodel?

It depends on your layout, your household and your long-term plans. Some homeowners want the openness of a walk-in shower; others want the comfort of a tub, and a primary suite can hold both. For costs, pros and cons, see our tub-to-shower conversion guide and our walk-in shower vs. tub guide.

Can you help with bathroom design and finish selections?

Yes. We guide you through layout, tile, vanities, countertops, fixtures, lighting and mirrors so the finished room fits the rest of the house, with renderings before anything is ordered. The $250 design retainer is credited once you sign.

Do you offer financing for bathroom remodeling projects?

Financing is available through Hearth (NMLS ID 1628533), which works with a network of lending partners, including 0% APR promotional options for qualified homeowners. Checking your options takes about a minute and uses a soft credit check that does not affect your score. Posada is not a lender; rates and terms are set by the lending partner.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Clermont?

Only for some work. Rules vary by jurisdiction: in Orange County, for example, moving plumbing, electrical or walls needs a permit, while new finished flooring does not. Inside Clermont city limits, permits go through the City of Clermont Building Services; addresses outside the city limits go through Lake County. Permits included when your project needs one; we coordinate it through our licensed general contractor partner. Our Florida remodel permit guide covers the details.
